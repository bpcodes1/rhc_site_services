# SEO Glossary (plain English)

Reference for explaining SEO terms simply. Written for Enrique to learn from
and to use when explaining the work to Rafa, Bryan, or clients.

## The big picture: Google is a librarian

Google's robot walks around reading every page on the internet (crawling). It
files the pages it likes into its giant library (indexing). When a person asks
a question ("porta potty rental portland"), the librarian pulls the pages it
thinks answer best and lists them in order (ranking) on the results page (the
SERP). The words the person typed are the keyword. All of SEO is one goal:
when someone asks the librarian about your topic, your page is one of the
first ones handed over.

## Words about your pages

- Meta title: the clickable headline for your page in Google's list. The
  strongest single signal you control.
- Meta description: the two gray lines under that headline. Doesn't affect
  ranking; it's your mini advertisement to earn the click.
- H1: the big headline on the page itself, the one visitors see.
- Canonical tag: a hidden label saying "THIS is my official address." Stops
  Google from getting confused when copies of a page exist.
- Sitemap: a list of all your pages handed to Google, like a table of contents.
- robots.txt: a note taped to your front door telling robots what they may read.
- Schema (structured data): labels in the code stating plain facts for
  machines: "this is a business, phone is X, it serves these cities."
- Internal links: your pages linking to each other. Hallways in a building;
  they guide visitors and pass importance between pages.
- Anchor text: the clickable words of a link. Google reads them as a hint
  about the destination.
- Doorway pages: cheap copy-paste city pages with just the name swapped.
  Google punishes these. Real local content (permit guides, local rules) is
  what makes a city page legitimate.

## Words about trust and reputation

- Backlink: another website linking to yours. Each one is a vote: "this site
  is worth recommending."
- Citations / NAP: NAP = Name, Address, Phone. Citations are places that list
  them (Yelp, BBB, Facebook). They must match everywhere or Google trusts
  you less.
- Brand authority: how much people know and search for your name
  specifically. The #1 protection when Google changes its rules. New sites
  start at zero and build it.
- E-E-A-T: Google's checklist for "is this a real, honest, experienced
  business?" Proven with real photos, real people, contact info, legal pages,
  and reviews. Shown, not claimed.

## Words about local businesses

- Google Business Profile (GBP): your business card inside Google Maps. For
  local searches it matters as much as the whole website.
- Local pack (map pack): the box with a map and 3 businesses at the top of
  local results. You can only win it near your real verified address, which
  is why distant cities are a regular-results play, not a map play.

## Words about the machinery

- 301 redirect: a permanent mail-forwarding order. "I moved; send visitors
  and my reputation to the new address."
- Client-side rendering / prerendering: this site is like flat-packed
  furniture; it assembles itself in the browser using JavaScript. Google can
  assemble it (slowly). Social media and most AI robots cannot; they see an
  empty box. Prerendering ships the furniture already built.
- Core Web Vitals: Google's measurements of how fast a page loads and how
  quickly it responds to taps.
- Soft 404: a missing page that pretends everything is OK instead of saying
  "not found." Confuses robots.

## Words about strategy

- Authority tier: the level of competition your site can currently win at.
  Sports leagues: win the small league first; each win earns promotion.
  Expecting to win the big-city keyword on day one is skipping leagues.
- Long-tail keywords: longer, more specific searches ("2 stall restroom
  trailer rental bend oregon"). Fewer searchers, much easier wins, and how a
  new site earns its first victories.
- AI Overviews / GEO: the AI-written answers at the top of most searches now.
  GEO is writing so the AI picks YOU as one of its few quoted sources.
- CTR: of the people who saw you in results, how many clicked you.
